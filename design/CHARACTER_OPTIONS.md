## Three alternate origins

These are mutually exclusive **starting biographies**, not three chapters to quietly combine. Each is broad fictional mercenary material, with no named faction, planet, canon battle or claim about MW5 history. None is adopted or committed to memory.

**Origin 1 — The salvage hand who bought a seat**  
Her first trade was recovering useful machinery from other people's expensive mistakes. She saved for training and a place on a mercenary crew because she wanted a say in which mistakes got made. She respects maintenance, hates waste and measures loyalty by who stays to help when the profitable part is over. Her edge is practical: bravado still has to survive the repair bill.

**Origin 2 — The security officer who would not sign**  
Her first career was in a private security company. She lost her position after refusing to sign a readiness report she believed was false. She entered mercenary work convinced that honest disagreement is more useful than obedient theater. She likes a clear chain of responsibility and keeps her own promises. Her tension is learning when she has made her point and needs to let another capable person decide.

**Origin 3 — The courier who wanted a larger horizon**  
Her first career was carrying ordinary freight between scattered settlements. Curiosity drew her toward harder work; she put herself through training and took the first serious mercenary offer. She brings impatience, curiosity and a habit of finding something amusing in a miserable shift. She enjoys calculated risk, but her restless streak has to coexist with people who expect her to come back.

The first favors practical dry humor; the second favors direct disagreement; the third allows more mischief. Any can still use style A, B or C. Fictional training or expertise never substitutes for missing loadout data or native game evidence.

## Original voice directions — text only

No performer, clone, provider or audio model is proposed. These describe possible delivery for an original female character; none has been synthesized, heard, measured or selected.

**Quiet steel**  
A comfortable low-to-middle register, steady pacing and clean consonants. Let the wit sit in a small pause rather than a sneer. She sounds like she has finished deciding whether to panic. Combat compresses the phrasing; it does not turn every sentence into a growl. Keep the voice natural rather than forcing a rasp.

**Knife-edge grin**  
A clear middle register with quicker conversational turns and a trace of amusement. The smile belongs in the timing, not a laugh after every joke. She enjoys testing an argument and can sound warmly impressed when someone earns it. Danger or a possible casualty removes the smile immediately; the words become plain and careful.

**Hard stop**  
A grounded middle register, economical emphasis and decisive sentence endings. She is willing to leave a silence after an objection. The force comes from certainty about what she knows, never volume or pretending to know more. Give her a little warmth when the disagreement is over so she remains a partner rather than a permanent drill instructor.

Pitch and delivery are separate from how sarcastic the writing is. A quieter voice can deliver B; a firm voice can deliver A. Actual intelligibility, comfort and audio preference need a later audition.
