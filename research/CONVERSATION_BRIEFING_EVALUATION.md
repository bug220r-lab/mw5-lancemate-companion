## Minimum representation invariants

1. **Change serialization, not observations.** Keep the original context frozen and retain the existing projector as the actor/metric authority. The formatter receives that validated result and explicitly supplied settings; it must not derive ownership or facts from request text, personality, case ID, evaluation metadata or history. Use generic field-to-briefing rules, not case-specific answers. The resulting briefing is model input, not an authored radio response or a new game adapter.

2. **State identity and its absence plainly.** Distinguish the context's bound internal actor from its configured callsign. An unset callsign means no setting was supplied, not that an in-game assignment was independently checked. Profile keys remain style selection, not actor identity or spoken names. If actor binding is absent, say that the context does not identify which listed actor is speaking; do not silently choose a row. Known binding with no own reading must instead say the reading was not supplied for that actor.

3. **Preserve semantic scope.** Separate own, other listed actors and unattributed rows. “Other” does not mean teammate, enemy, visible, alive or controllable. Render only literal supported `heat` labels and **selected-weapon** ammunition availability. Do not turn low heat into safety, high heat into shutdown, available ammunition into a loaded/reloaded weapon, or an empty selected weapon into total incapacity. No new units, thresholds, counts, routes, action outcomes or freshness claims.

4. **Describe uncertainty without resolving it.** Missing readings, missing binding, `CONFLICT` and `UNSUPPORTED_EVIDENCE` need distinct wording. Preserve every conflicting alternative and the fact that no winner was selected; do not prefer row order, newer-looking IDs or matching personality. An unsupported metric cannot be presented as resolved because one supported alternative looks convenient. Agreeing duplicate entries do not establish independent corroboration. Unknown-actor rows stay separate instead of becoming one imaginary unnamed pilot.

5. **Keep unsupported material and history visibly untrusted.** Preserve every existing `unprojectedFacts` payload and both original generated histories, including hostile transcript text. Do not translate `heatState` into supported `heat`, infer a binding from fact-level `speakerActorId`, summarize contradictory data away, or turn earlier generated speech into an event. Raw material may remain escaped JSON/quoted data within an otherwise readable briefing. Keep request text in its own untrusted section; quotes/newlines inside supplied strings must not create new system-role messages or trusted section headings.

6. **Retain traceability without asking the model to speak source labels.** Full original facts, projection statuses, source references/pointers, supplied timestamps and the exact rendered briefing belong in the receipt. Compact labels such as `s0` may stay audit-only if each rendered supported observation maps back to its unchanged source set. Removing decorative labels cannot remove adverse evidence, resolve conflicts or authenticate observations. A synthetic timestamp is not proof of live freshness.

A suitable generic order is: speaker binding and configured callsign; own reported states or explicitly missing/unresolved readings; other listed actors; unattributed observations; unsupported raw material; unverified, possibly undelivered generated history; the user request. Headings and data descriptions are permitted; supplying a case's desired radio answer is not. Never assert negative gameplay events merely because receipts are absent: “no supplied confirmation” differs from “the order was not accepted.”
